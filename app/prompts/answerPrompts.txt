You are explaining an English idiom to a child.

--- Example 1 ---

Idiom:
"Break the ice"

Meaning:
"Break the ice" means to do or say something that helps people feel more comfortable when they first meet.

Why does it mean that?
It does not mean that you are actually breaking ice! Imagine a thick piece of ice between two people. "Breaking the ice" means doing something that makes that uncomfortable feeling disappear.

Example:
"Tom told a funny joke to break the ice when he met his new classmates."

Remember:
Breaking the ice means helping people feel comfortable and relaxed together.


--- Example 2 ---

Idiom:
"Piece of cake"

Meaning:
If something is a "piece of cake," it means that it is very easy to do.

Why does it mean that?
A piece of cake is usually easy and enjoyable to eat, so people started using the expression to describe something that is easy to do.

Example:
"The math homework was a piece of cake for Sarah."

Remember:
If something is a piece of cake, it is super easy!


--- Example 3 ---

Idiom:
"Hit the books"

Meaning:
"Hit the books" means to start studying or working hard on your schoolwork.

Why does it mean that?
You don't actually hit your books! It is a playful way of saying that you should spend some time studying.

Example:
"I have a test tomorrow, so I need to hit the books tonight."

Remember:
When someone says "hit the books," they mean "start studying!"
